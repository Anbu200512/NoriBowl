Our Origin

It started with a borrowed stockpot

Kenji Nakamura spent a decade in Fukuoka ramen shops — washing bones at dawn, skimming pots at midnight. In 2014 he rented a shuttered fishmonger on Harbour Road, laid ten stools along a single plank of timber and opened with one bowl on the board: Hakata tonkotsu.

The first winter was quiet. Then a food blogger wrote six sentences about the broth, and by the following Saturday there was a queue around the block. We have never taken the pot off since.

Broth Preparation

Eighteen hours, told in four acts

  1. 01

    Roast & rinse

    Femurs roast until the edges caramelise, then get pressure-rinsed so the stock stays clean and sweet.

  2. 02

    Rolling simmer

    A hard, steady boil emulsifies marrow and collagen into that signature ivory colour — never a lazy bubble.

  3. 03

    Hourly skimming

    Someone stands over the pot all day. Foam comes off by hand so the broth never turns grey or heavy.

  4. 04

    Season to order

    Tare goes into the empty bowl, broth is ladled over it, noodles drop in — ninety seconds from pot to pass.

Noodle-making

Milled, kneaded, rested overnight

We import wheat from Hokkaido and mill part of it in-house. Kansui water gives the dough its spring; a cold overnight rest gives it memory. Each morning the cutter sets three cuts — thin straight for tonkotsu, flat for shoyu, thick for mazesoba.

What We Value

Three promises we keep daily

Patience over shortcuts

If the broth needs another hour, service waits an hour. Nothing powder-based ever touches our pots.

Hospitality without theatre

Warm towels and big smiles are fine, but the real welcome is a bowl that arrives exactly how you ordered it.

A neighbourhood shop

Regulars get the nod, first-timers get the explanation, and everyone gets the same carefully made bowl.
